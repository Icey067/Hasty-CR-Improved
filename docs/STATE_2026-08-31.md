# Where things stand, 2026-08-31

Written so the findings below do not have to be reconstructed from a chat log.
Three of them change what "a good checkpoint" means, so read the first section
before picking one to play.

## The finding that matters most

**Simulation win rate and placement quality are decoupled, and placement is
what transfers to ladder.** Measured against `data/vod/plays.jsonl`, which is
where a strong player actually puts each card, taken from recorded footage:

| policy | median offset from the pro median | spread ratio | cards it plays |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clone_pilot` — the one that pushes trophies | **5.4 tiles** | **0.68** | 7 |
| `physA_best` — 92.0% held out in simulation | 7.9 tiles | 0.48 | 5 |

PPO bought its win rate by collapsing placement. `physA_best` moved Ice Golem
and Ice Spirit from tile y=24, in front of its own towers, to **(9, 31)** — the
back line behind its own king tower, about thirteen tiles from where the
recorded player puts them — and stopped playing Fireball and Log often enough
to measure at all. A spread ratio of 0.48 means it varies its placement half as
much as a person does: it drops the card on the same tile whatever the board.

The owner's description of the live bot, before any of this was measured, was
*"places ice golem at king tower behind it"*. That is this table.

None of it is visible in the number the supervisor selects on, so
`scripts/rl_supervisor.py` now prints it beside the held-out win rate at every
audit. **Report only.** Optimising a placement histogram would produce a policy
standing in the right places for the wrong reasons, which is the same error as
training on a win rate wearing a different hat — `scripts/placement_score.py`
argues that at length.

Judge a checkpoint on both numbers. A high win rate with an offset near 8 and a
spread near 0.4 is the failure mode, not a result.

**How much weight this carries — corrected the same evening.** Placement swings
widely between checkpoints of one run, so a single reading is a weaker claim
than it first appears. Across `pathfixB`'s surviving snapshots:

| step | offset | spread |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2M | 11.6 | 0.47 |
| 3.7M | 4.8 | 0.62 |
| 5.7M (promoted) | 13.4 | 0.45 |
| 7.2M | 6.8 | 0.64 |

The measurement is deterministic for a given checkpoint and seed - the same
weights re-measure identically - so that is real variation between checkpoints
rather than sampling noise. But it is computed over the cards clearing a
15-placement threshold, so a card dropping below it changes which cards the
median covers, and the number moves for a reason that is not about placement.

So: the direction of the clone-versus-`physA` result matches what the owner
described of the live bot, and is worth acting on. The precision first written
here - two single readings, 5.4 against 7.9, presented as a clean gap - is more
than the measurement earns. Treat placement as a flag to investigate a
checkpoint, not as a figure to rank two of them by.

**The defect this did expose is real and separate.** `pathfixB` was promoted at
step 5.7M on eval score. At 3.7M it scored slightly lower and placed 4.8 tiles
out against the promoted checkpoint's 13.4 - and 3.7M has since been pruned by
the league retention, so it no longer exists. Selection kept the worse policy
and threw away the better one, and nothing in the run recorded that it had.

## Engine corrections

Both came out of driving `Jason-XII/clash-royale-simulator` as an oracle on
identical scenarios. Neither was found by a test here, and both had been
reported by the owner and not reproduced.

**Movement speed was 20% too slow for every unit.** `Speed` in the shipped data
is millitiles per tick at the client's 20Hz, not tiles per minute. Slow/Medium/
Fast/Very Fast are 0.90/1.20/1.80/2.40 tiles per second. Movement is the only
quantity in the engine measured against the clock — hit speed, load time and
projectile flight are all in real milliseconds — so this changed damage taken
per tile walked by 20% for every interaction in the game. Full evidence in
`docs/SPEED_SCALE_CORRECTION.md`.

**Troops walked the wrong way for three seconds before turning around.** The
distance field counted steps with eight-way movement, which is a Chebyshev
metric: from (7,23) toward a goal at (3,16) the distance is `max(4,7) = 7`, and
stepping *sideways* to (8,23) is also 7. The sideways move was free, every tile
where `|dy| > |dx|` was a tie, and ties were broken by the order of a tuple
whose first entry was `(1, 0)` — right. Now a weighted field, 10 straight and
14 diagonal, which is the reference's cost model. Sideways path error against
that engine fell from 2.5 tiles to 0.7.

Every endpoint comparison agreed through all of this, because both engines
always reached the same tower. `tools/path_trace.py` watches the route instead,
and `tests/test_pathfinding_drift.py` pins it.

## Perception, which was worse than the policy

**The bot was blind to eleven cards.** `YoloVision` resolves each detected class
against the vendored BuildABot unit table, which holds 97 units; the detector
knows 201 classes. Everything added to the game since that table was written was
detected and silently dropped. On a frame saved during a live match, a **Goblin
Giant at 0.92 confidence standing in our half beside our own Cannon** was thrown
away, and the observation handed to the policy read `theirs=0`. It held its
elixir, did not answer with Fireball, and put a cheap unit at the back — correct
play for the board it was shown.

An unresolvable class is now counted and logged as `VISION_BLIND`, and
`tests/test_vision_coverage.py` fails if any detector class falls through
unnamed.

**Capture was the largest cost in the decision loop.** 169ms a frame at 540x960,
upscaled bilinearly to the calibrated 1080x1920 — so the detector and the card
reader were reading an upscale of half-resolution frames. An on-device H.264
stream gives 32ms at native 1080x1920. Opt-in via `--stream-capture`; it falls
back to screencap on any failure, including its own ~2s warm-up.

## Open, and what would close it

**Targeting.** Five of twelve scenarios disagree with the reference, in the most
frequent situation in the game: something hitting a tower when a defender
arrives. Ours retargets onto the defender; theirs holds the tower. Nothing is
changed, because which is correct is genuinely unsettled — Clash Royale
retargets on a short timer, which argues for ours; the reference's rule is
deliberate and that engine is reported as behaving correctly, which argues for
theirs. **One clip settles it:** a Musketeer hitting a tower when a Knight walks
into her range, and whether her next shot lands on the tower or the Knight.
`tools/target_diff.py` has the table.

**Body blocking.** Unblocked now reproduces the recording exactly at 7 hits, and
blocked is 3 against a recorded 2. But ours is nearly flat across block timing
(3, 3, 4, 4) where the reference swings hard (6, 5, 2, 2), and real blocking is
sharply timing-sensitive — so being one hit away while flat is being close for
the wrong reason. Four approaches have now failed and are recorded at
`BODY_BLOCK_STEP_PCT` in `sim/engine.py`, including two tried today. The
remaining suspicion is that steering has no path-length cost.

**The RoyaleAPI scrape.** Blocked on one sign-in. `/data/replay` is login-gated
and the scraper drives its own browser profile, so signing in elsewhere does not
carry. It found 451 rated players on our archetype spanning rating 3552 to 2309.
Run `tools/royale_scrape.py` and sign in *in the window it opens*. Cloudflare
now serves an interactive checkbox that does not self-solve; that is not
automated here on purpose.

## Checkpoints

`checkpoints/physA_best.pt` and `physB_best.pt` carry manifests with their
held-out numbers. Both predate the pathfinding fix, so they learned a game where
troops walk sideways for three seconds. `pathfixA` and `pathfixB` are the
successors, training on the corrected engine with selection scored on the
**worst** of the rule engine and meta decks rather than one of them — because
every policy this project has produced was best against exactly whatever it was
selected on, and that is one failure, not two.
